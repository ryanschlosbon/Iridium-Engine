# M5.8 Reflection-Probe Selection Contract — 2026-08-11

Status: backend-neutral component, stable persistence/editor integration, world
extraction, CPU reference selection, GPU-facing record publication, Vulkan clustered
assignment, and shared deferred/complex-forward local-specular sampling are
implemented. Backend-neutral cubemap orientation, capture scheduling, invalidation,
partial-capture isolation, and complete-only publication are also implemented;
Vulkan capture-target allocation and last-known-good lifetime separation are also
implemented. Scene capture/convolution and final qualification remain.

## Behavior and architecture

- `ReflectionProbeComponent` freezes sphere/box influence shapes, interior blend
  distance, intensity/priority, baked/on-demand/realtime update intent, optional box
  projection, capture resolution and clip range, sky inclusion, and stable cooked
  environment identity. Transform remains the sole owner of position/orientation.
- Runtime selection rejects disabled, invalid, missing, or nonresident products.
  Influencing probes are ordered by priority, influence, smallest volume, then
  stable scene UUID. The working set is bounded to four candidates. The top two
  share the covered local weight while the remaining boundary weight goes to the
  global environment, avoiding a hard switch as local influence approaches zero.
  An empty result explicitly selects the global environment fallback.
- Sphere and oriented-box influence use an interior boundary fade. Box projection
  intersects the local reflection ray with the influence box and returns a corrected
  world direction; invalid/outside inputs retain a safe normalized direction.
- The code is renderer/backend neutral and introduces no Vulkan, ImGui, file-dialog,
  or source-JSON dependency.

## GPU publication and clustered-assignment checkpoint

- `PackedGpuReflectionProbe` freezes a std430-compatible 112-byte record: rigid
  world-to-probe transform, sphere radius or box extents plus blend distance, world
  position/intensity, shape/parallax flags, and an abstract environment-table slot.
  The record contains neither Vulkan descriptors nor persistent asset identities.
- `ReflectionProbePublisher` admits resident probes deterministically by priority,
  smaller influence volume, then stable UUID. Its initial capacity is 64 records and
  its guarded maximum is 4096. Owner UUIDs retain transient slots across frames;
  removed slots are reusable. Record revisions, an active-list revision, coalesced
  changed ranges, and changed-byte counts allow incremental backend uploads.
- Missing residency, unresolved environment-table entries, and capacity omissions
  are distinct diagnostics. Incomplete probes never enter the active list, and an
  omitted local probe therefore preserves the global-environment fallback.
- `ClusteredReflectionProbeAssigner` remains a deterministic CPU oracle for the GPU path.
  It reuses the 32x32x24 light-grid dimensions while retaining separate probe
  headers/indices, conservatively bounds spheres and oriented boxes, and publishes
  at most four candidates per cluster. Ordering is priority, cluster-center
  influence, smaller volume, then UUID; the per-pixel shading rule chooses the top
  two, apportions local coverage between them, and gives residual weight to global.
- The global reference guard is 1,048,576 entries. At native 4K the accepted grid is
  120x68x24 (195,840 clusters), so four entries per cluster require at most 783,360
  references and remain within that bound. If a custom configuration exceeds it,
  later clusters publish empty or partial bounded headers, report overflow, and use
  the global environment instead of reading an invalid index.
- Production frame preparation now performs probe extraction, environment residency
  resolution, and stable publication, with counters for extracted/active/nonresident/
  unresolved/omitted probes and changed bytes. The CPU clustered assigner is an
  executable reference contract, not a per-frame production workload.

## Vulkan publication and shading checkpoint

- Each frame owns host-visible record, active-slot, and parameter buffers plus
  device-local cluster-header and cluster-index buffers. Record revisions and the
  active-list revision limit uploads to changed ranges; capacity growth occurs only
  at a fence-safe frame boundary.
- One compute dispatch assigns at most four probe slots to every existing light-grid
  cluster. It conservatively bounds sphere and oriented-box volumes, preserves the
  CPU contract's deterministic priority/influence ordering, and publishes empty
  headers when no valid local environment applies or the guarded global reference
  capacity is exhausted.
- Scene descriptor bindings 28 through 30 expose probe records, headers, and indices.
  Binding 31 is a fixed 64-entry prefiltered cubemap table. The backend validates
  compatible cooked RGBA16F cubemaps and fills every unused entry with the neutral
  black cube, so missing residency never leaves a stale or invalid descriptor.
- The shared environment include selects the strongest two of the four cluster
  candidates per pixel, applies optional box projection, and samples no more than
  two indexed local prefiltered cubemaps per specular lobe. Standard deferred and
  complex-forward base/clearcoat/sheen/iridescence paths consume the same function.
  The local coverage is blended with global prefiltered radiance, retaining a clean
  boundary transition. Diffuse irradiance deliberately remains global in this
  reflection-probe slice.
- The production environment table is backend neutral at the RHI boundary: scene
  publication contains abstract environment slots, while Vulkan owns descriptors.
  Up to 4,096 probe records may share the current 64 resident local environments.

At native 4K, the 120x68x24 grid requires 195,840 eight-byte headers and at most
783,360 four-byte indices. With the initial 64-record capacity, active slots, and
the 256-byte parameter block, the logical buffer payload is 4,707,840 bytes
(4.49 MiB) per frame, or 8.98 MiB across the two frames in flight. This excludes
the cooked environment images themselves, which are existing persistent environment
resources and may be shared by multiple probes.

## Capture scheduling and publication checkpoint

- `buildReflectionProbeCaptureFaces` freezes six 90-degree right-handed cameras in
  +X/-X/+Y/-Y/+Z/-Z order. Its up vectors and deliberately non-inverted projection
  reproduce `vulkan_rhs_+x_-x_+y_-y_+z_-z_v1`, the existing cooked environment
  convention, rather than introducing a runtime-only cube orientation.
- `ReflectionProbeCaptureScheduler` owns only stable scene UUIDs and renderer-neutral
  requests. Priority, then lower resolution, then UUID provide deterministic budget
  ordering. The default frame budget is six 512-square faces (1,572,864 texels), at
  most six faces for one probe per frame, and four captures in flight. These are
  policy defaults, not hard renderer limits.
- Baked probes do no runtime work without an explicit capture request. OnDemand
  probes capture a missing product and react to explicit requests, capture-setting/
  transform changes, or pipeline changes; scene/light/environment revisions alone
  retain their prior product. Realtime probes additionally invalidate on those
  dependency revisions.
- Faces may be rendered across frames into private staging storage. The scheduler
  tracks an opaque monotonic ticket and face mask, never calls a partial cube
  sampleable, and keeps a previous complete product sampleable while its replacement
  is in flight. Completing all six faces exposes a publication request, but the new
  capture does not become current until the future convolution/upload path explicitly
  acknowledges fence-safe publication. Abandoning a failed refresh preserves the
  last-known-good product.
- Removed owners retire their transient scheduling state. Duplicate owners, invalid
  modes/resolutions/projections/revisions, completion mismatches, stale tickets, and
  publication before all faces complete fail explicitly.

This checkpoint is not connected to production frame submission and therefore adds
no GPU pass, persistent VRAM, or steady-frame CPU cost. It defines the boundary that
the Vulkan scene-radiance capture and environment convolution path will consume.

## Vulkan capture-target lifetime checkpoint

- `VulkanReflectionProbeCaptureTargets` realizes each active capture as separate
  cube-compatible RGBA16F raw-radiance, six-layer D32 depth, and full-mip RGBA16F
  storage/sampled prefilter images. Six raw/depth render views and one six-layer
  storage view per prefilter mip make the eventual graphics-to-compute handoff
  explicit.
- Stable owner/ticket lookup rejects stale or duplicate work. Abandon destroys only
  private staging; fence-safe promotion moves the completed prefilter image into the
  owner's last-known-good slot and retires the former published product. Callers must
  perform acquire, abandon, and promotion only at fence-safe submission boundaries.
- The pool guards device cube dimensions, 64 stable owners, four concurrent
  captures, 3 GiB of aggregate logical staging, and a separate 4 GiB published-
  product budget. An authored tier is either honored or rejected with a diagnostic;
  it is never silently reduced.

  | Tier | Mips | Private staging | Published prefilter |
  |---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | 128 | 8 | 2.125 MiB | 1.000 MiB |
  | 512 | 10 | 34.000 MiB | 16.000 MiB |
  | 1024 | 11 | 136.000 MiB | 64.000 MiB |
  | 2048 | 12 | 544.000 MiB | 256.000 MiB |
  | 4096 | 13 | 2.125 GiB | 1.000 GiB |

  These are format-level logical byte counts; Vulkan allocation alignment and memory
  commitment can be higher. The 4096 tier is deliberately a guarded cinematic tier,
  not a default realtime choice.
- The validation diagnostic performs a real 128-tier allocation, creates every face
  and mip view, promotes it, starts and abandons a replacement while retaining the
  published product, then removes the owner. All ownership and logical-byte counters
  return to zero without Vulkan validation errors.

The pool is initialized in production but owns no images in ordinary steady state,
so this checkpoint adds no capture GPU pass or persistent VRAM until a scheduler is
wired to it.

## Persistence, authoring, and extraction checkpoint

- `iridium.component.reflection_probe` is registered with stable source and cooked
  schema identities. Canonical JSON and cooked scenes retain sphere/box influence,
  blend/intensity/priority, baked/on-demand/realtime intent, parallax mode,
  128/256/512/1024/2048/4096 capture tiers, clip range, sky inclusion, and the
  optional environment asset GUID. Resolved publication state and diagnostics remain
  transient.
- The Inspector exposes the component through the M4 component registry and command
  transaction path. Environment assignment uses the typed HDRI drag payload and
  cooked thumbnail; add/remove/property mutations remain undoable and do not embed
  ImGui behavior in runtime component data.
- World extraction emits candidates in stable UUID order, derives a rigid oriented
  transform from `TransformComponent::worldMatrix`, deliberately removes authored or
  inherited scale from probe influence, omits invalid/missing transforms with typed
  diagnostics, and retains nonresident candidates at zero influence. Residency may
  be supplied by the runtime asset system or inferred from matching publication
  state.
- The intentional runtime-manifest expansion supersedes the M5.8 frozen M4/M5 fixture
  contracts. Canonical source hashes are unchanged; cooked manifest/cook/artifact
  hashes and byte counts were re-frozen by the deterministic contract tests.
