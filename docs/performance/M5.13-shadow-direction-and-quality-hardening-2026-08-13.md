# M5.13 Shadow direction and quality hardening — 2026-08-13

Status: implemented and verified as post-acceptance M5 corrective hardening.

## Corrected behavior

- A light's transformed local `+Z` axis is now its authored emission direction in
  extraction, CPU/GPU cluster assignment, spot projection, directional cascades,
  and direct shading. The light gizmo already visualized `+Z`, so spot and
  directional illumination now follows the visible arrow/cone instead of pointing
  backward. Persisted transforms and the Light v2 schema are unchanged; scenes
  rotated around the former bug may need a deliberate 180-degree authoring fix.
- Directional shadows default to 4096 per cascade with a 0.85 practical/log split,
  concentrating more texels near the camera. The persistent two-owner D32 array is
  512 MiB, up from the accepted 128 MiB 2048 product.
- The spot atlas defaults to 8192, and per-light spot qualities map to
  512/1024/2048/4096. The atlas is 256 MiB D32, up from 64 MiB at 4096. Its default
  update budget is 16 MiTexels, enough to refresh one Ultra tile per frame.
- Ultra PCSS uses 24 blocker and 48 filter samples; Cinematic uses 32/64. Kernel
  rotation varies per screen pixel rather than per shadow texel, preventing a
  visible block pattern in which neighboring receiver pixels shared one orientation.
- Directional, spot, and point receiver bias is measured from the receiver's
  world-space shadow-texel footprint. This replaces the former fixed normalized
  depth/fixed-centimeter offsets, which could displace a directional receiver by
  tens of centimeters because each cascade carries a large caster-depth guard.
  Normal-mapped shading normals no longer move the receiver position in the
  shadow projection, so material detail cannot pull a shadow away from its caster.
- Hard and contact shadow edges no longer expose one nearest raw depth texel.
  Directional and spot maps reconstruct compare results bilinearly under a compact
  3x3 tent; point cubes use a stable dense disk because interpolating nonlinear
  projected cube depth before comparison is invalid. Broad PCSS penumbrae retain
  the existing physical contact-hardening path.
- Point cubes retain the accepted 1024 Ultra face ceiling and pool capacities, but
  the raw projected-depth sampler is nearest. Manual PCF/PCSS performs the filtering;
  interpolating nonlinear raw depth before comparison was the source of stretched
  cube-face artifacts.
- New Light components start at useful physical authoring values: 100,000 lux for
  directional, 10,000 candela for local lights, and 0.05 m source radius. Existing
  explicitly persisted values and old missing-field/migration defaults are not
  rewritten. The Inspector uses logarithmic physical-unit controls and Moon,
  Overcast, Sun, Bulb, Studio, and Hero presets.

These changes preserve ADR-0007's accepted `1e-4` photometric-to-scene calibration.
The intensity correction is an authoring-range and creation-default correction, not
an arbitrary renderer multiplier.

## Verification checkpoint

- Debug and Release build successfully; shader compilation is clean.
- Debug and Release CTest pass 69/69 after adding an explicit
  `m5_13CorrectiveHardening` supersession. Historical M5.9/M5.11 hashes remain
  unchanged and are resolved through the new current-contract hash table.
- Release Vulkan-validation captures at 1920x1080 complete for directional, two
  opposing spot, and two opposing point fixtures. The directional capture reports
  one owner, four sampleable cascades, 4096 resolution, and no omitted owner. The
  local captures exercise independent lights under the shared clustered path.
- A short profiled RTX 4090 point-fixture run reports 512 MiB directional and
  592 MiB local persistent shadow residency. The combined 1,104 MiB reservation is
  576 MiB above M5.11's accepted 128 + 400 MiB conventional-map reservation; this
  is the explicit cost of the 4096 directional and 8192 spot defaults.
- Final Ultra captures are under `out/captures/m5_13_final_directional`,
  `out/captures/m5_13_final_spot`, and `out/captures/m5_13_final_point`.
- The later receiver-bias/hard-edge corrective captures are under
  `out/captures/m5_13_bias_filter_directional_v2`,
  `out/captures/m5_13_bias_filter_spot`, and
  `out/captures/m5_13_bias_filter_point_final`; the explicit hard-shadow check is
  under `out/captures/m5_13_hard_directional`. All ran with Khronos validation;
  the explicit zero-diameter directional capture verifies the hard-shadow path.
  The first 0.35-texel experiment was rejected after its capture showed planar

The validation fixtures are deliberately simple contact-shadow scenes and do not
replace a matched native-4K car comparison. This checkpoint therefore does not
claim a new five-process production performance baseline. It records the material
persistent-memory increase and leaves dressed-scene 4K timing comparison required
before changing the accepted M5 frame-budget baseline.

## Remaining evolution

Conventional maps still exhibit finite projection resolution, especially for a
large point-light range or extreme grazing receiver angles. ADR-0010 keeps sparse
virtual shadow maps, screen-space contact supplementation, and M9 temporal stability
in later milestones. Those paths must retain the common `+Z` emission convention,
per-light visibility, and safe cache fallback established here.

The point fixture also demonstrates that projected cubemap depth is highly
sensitive at receiver/cube-face grazing angles. Its bias is now distance- and
resolution-scaled rather than a fixed centimeter, but a general normal-offset
strategy using a geometric (not normal-mapped) receiver normal remains the next
point-specific quality task. It should be validated on the user's dressed car
before treating the synthetic flat receiver as a complete point-shadow oracle.
