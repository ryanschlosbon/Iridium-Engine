  `30252593f8fdd2de5dffbb8da31bb570ff49a7c0`; this matches the accepted M4.0

- Fixed seed `5570198094586900512`; one warmup and 30 measured samples per case.
- The version-3 result contains all 34 M4.0 cases plus seven M4.1 cases. Cooked

- Registry contract: 64-bit index/generation handles, dense component vectors,

## Interpretation and decision

The first implementation keyed every sparse component map by the full 64-bit
handle. Measurement exposed broad 37-114% lookup regressions. M4.1 therefore keeps
the dense handle as generation authority but keys the sparse map by the 32-bit
entity index. Every lookup validates the resolved dense handle before access. This
preserves stale safety and materially improves existing hit/view/sort paths.

The remaining regressions are explained and bounded:

- creation/recycle maintains generation, alive, and free-list state;
- deletion moves and stores 64-bit dense handles;
- hierarchy breadth and selection add full-handle validation to every lookup;
- miss probes use the safe non-mutating lookup rather than the former unchecked
  `operator[]` access;
- authoring JSON save/load remains within +4.4% while retaining its legacy-v0
  behavior for the M4.2/M4.3 transition.

Dense iteration, the principal runtime data path, changes by +1.9%. The sparse miss,
breadth hierarchy, selection, and structural allocation costs remain named M4.8
candidates. They do not justify an archetype rewrite and do not block this
correctness-required identity slice.
