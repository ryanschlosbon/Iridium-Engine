# M7.6 large-occluded-workload admission — 2026-09-12

## Result

The fused previous-frame Hi-Z route passes its remaining controlled native-4K
performance gate and M7.6 is accepted as a workload-selectable implementation.
The frozen fixture removes 5,133 of 6,464 opaque commands (79.4090%) after
frustum compaction. Across the final matched pair, median GBuffer GPU time falls
from 0.741376 ms to 0.081920 ms and median complete GPU time falls from
2.133760 ms to 1.471840 ms. The current-build baseline and rejection scene
captures are byte-identical.

The route remains default-off. One deliberately occlusion-heavy fixture proves
that the implementation scales when hidden raster work is material, but does not
justify paying the pyramid cost in every project or view. A later project policy
can select it by workload and view; uncertainty continues to fail visible.

## Controlled fixture

`assets/m7-occlusion-performance-manifest.v1.json` owns a 64-instance,
view-aligned depth stack of the representative Alfa Romeo asset. Normal
occludees retain unit scale and four-metre depth spacing. A manifest-owned
per-instance scale override enlarges only the nearest instance to 3x, producing
high coverage without multiplying the cost or bounds of every farther object.
The benchmark parser validates the override identity and finite nonzero scale,
and rejects it for the batched submission mode that cannot represent the
per-instance exception.

At 3840x2160, the fully instrumented validation route reports on every one of 16
measured frames:

- 6,464 requested candidates;
- 5,383 tested bounds;
- 1,081 exact `SmallBounds` fail-visible decisions;
- 5,133 exact would-reject and applied-reject decisions;
- 1,331 retained device commands;
- zero invalid results, unsafe mismatches, bin mismatches, or overflows.

The baseline and rejection captures at measured frame 10 both have SHA-256
`122c5bdf028b86fef300716214320d57bb6fb7ab3d85074e1ac47c73e0993dd1`.

## Deployable-path separation

Validation and `--depth-occlusion-qualification-oracle` retain the independent
CPU bounds projection, standalone device query, per-candidate fused result
readback, and exact safety/bin comparison. The ordinary rejection experiment
does not execute or allocate those qualification-only query streams, does not
read every fused result through host-visible memory, and suppresses fused result
writes. It consumes the compacted indirect counts directly while preserving
exact requested/retained/applied totals. Counters that require the independent
oracle are explicitly `unavailable`, never reported as exact zero.

This separation removes a measured host-readback regression: before the split,
the rejection run's median CPU frame was 8.6629 ms despite the GPU win. The final
deployable path records 4.8287 ms. Its only persistent allocation difference
from the pyramid-only baseline is a 32-byte dummy result binding per frame, or 64
requested/committed bytes total.

## Decision

The final M7.6 integration gate built every shader and target in Debug and Release;
both configurations passed all 77/77 CTests.

M7.6's conservative projection, live reduction, per-view temporal ownership,
camera-cut/depth-content/resize invalidation, small-object handling, fused
compaction, duplicate-storage removal, image parity, and controlled performance
gates are complete. No accepted ADR changed. The feature remains an explicit
workload choice while M7.7 becomes active for independent shadow/probe consumers
and conventional-shadow optimization.
