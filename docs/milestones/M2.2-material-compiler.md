# M2.2 Acceptance: Pure Material Compiler and Canonical Closure Math

- Status: Complete on 2026-07-22
- Milestone: M2 - Material Compiler and Canonical Shading Closures
- Source baseline: `30252593f8fdd2de5dffbb8da31bb570ff49a7c0`
- Worktree: intentionally dirty accepted M0/M1 and active M2 work
- Reference hardware: RTX 4090, Core i9-14900K, 64 GB system memory

## Decision

M2.2 is complete. Iridium now has a deterministic, pure CPU compiler from the M2.1
`SourceMaterial` contract to explicit standard, complex-forward, or unlit closure
recipes. The compiler has no RHI, Vulkan, editor, or active-renderer dependency.
`AssetManager`, the production graph, shaders, and the legacy GPU material remain
unchanged; M2.4 is still the first activation slice.

The compiler uses strict policy by default. Invalid source data, unsupported required
extensions, unsupported optional extensions in strict mode, conflicting workflows,
and invalid complex-lobe combinations fail without a compiled material. Permissive
editor policy may retain an unknown optional extension only by setting an explicit
fallback feature and diagnostic. No approximation is silent.

## Compiled architecture

`CompiledMaterial` schema version 1 contains:

- the selected metallic/roughness, archived specular/glossiness, or unlit workflow;
- standard closure factors for diffuse/F0/F90 evaluation, perceptual roughness,
  normal scale, AO, scene-linear emissive, alpha, and two-sided behavior;
- the full ordered symbolic texture program: semantic, source texture/image identity,
  channel mapping, transfer interpretation, UV set, transform, sampler, and per-use
  scalar;
- typed active complex-lobe records rather than an all-features structure;
- stable diagnostics and a SHA-256 semantic content hash independent of source
  material name/index.

The typed complex records are clearcoat, sheen, anisotropy, iridescence, thin
transmission, volume transmission, dispersion, and diffuse transmission. Each
retains its exact source factors and only its relevant texture mask. Clearcoat keeps
coat roughness and normal scale; iridescence keeps IOR and thickness range;
transmission keeps IOR and specular weight/color; volume keeps thickness and
attenuation data. These records establish the data boundary for M2.6 without claiming
that complex lighting or M6 transport has been implemented.

Active non-reducible lobes select `ComplexForward`. A lobe whose multiplicative
factor proves it identically zero reduces to the standard class and emits
`MATERIAL_DORMANT_EXTENSION`. BLEND without a complex lobe selects
`StandardForward`; MASK remains eligible for `StandardDeferred`. Unlit is a distinct
scene-linear closure and emits warnings for ignored lighting extensions.

## Canonical CPU reference evaluation

`evaluateMaterialSurface` evaluates selected texture samples and vertex color into:

- AP1 diffuse albedo and RGB F0;
- scalar F90/specular weight;
- authored perceptual roughness without the legacy 0.04 clamp;
- oriented world-space shading normal using tangent handedness and two-sided state;
- AO carried separately for later indirect lighting;
- AP1 scene-linear emissive;
- alpha and MASK coverage result;
- unlit AP1 radiance without a lighting BSDF.

Core metallic/roughness, IOR and `KHR_materials_specular`, and archived
specular/glossiness follow the equations frozen in the M2 plan and ADR-0001. Texture
RGB is decoded according to its use while alpha/data channels remain linear. Factors
are linear Rec.709/sRGB-primary values and are transformed to ACEScg/AP1 only after
factor/texture multiplication. Legal zero roughness remains zero in the closure;
the BSDF numerical alpha floor belongs to M2.5 evaluation, not compilation.

Transmission records emit a warning that production refraction and ordering remain
M6 work. Metallic-plus-transmission emits a separate suspicious-source warning
instead of inventing dielectric glass. Diffuse transmission names its remaining
M5/M6 transport dependency. Active volume without transmission, active dispersion
without transmission/volume, reversed iridescence thickness, and specular/glossiness
combined with IOR/specular extensions are deterministic errors.

## Automated verification

Complete Debug and Release builds succeed. Each configuration passes all 17 CTests.
`MaterialCompilerTests` covers:

- metallic/roughness factors plus encoded base-color and G/B texture samples;
- IOR and textured specular F0/F90 vectors;
- equivalent metallic/roughness and specular/glossiness closure vectors;
- zero roughness preservation;
- alpha, MASK cutoff, normal scale, tangent-space normal, AO, emissive strength, and
  AP1 conversion;
- two-sided normal orientation and unlit behavior;
- every complex-lobe class and dormant-lobe reduction;
- volume/dispersion/workflow conflicts and typed lobe data;
- strict and permissive unsupported-extension policies;
- NaN rejection and deterministic semantic hashes, including texture-operation hash
  sensitivity;
- all tracked runtime material fixtures and the optional sample car.

Tracked fixture results are exact: material lab compiles to three standard-deferred
and two complex-forward materials; the opaque emissive range compiles to six
standard-deferred materials; the M1 color-volume fixture compiles to twelve
standard-deferred and one complex-forward material.

The licensed car compiles all 87 materials: 80 standard-deferred, three
standard-forward, and four complex-forward. Paint materials 0 and 36 select typed
clearcoat complex closures. Window 6 remains the authored standard-forward metallic
BLEND surface; no glass behavior is invented. Lenses 3 and 4 select thin-transmission
complex closures and report metallic-suppression plus M6 transport limitations. The
car still has no authored emissive result.

## Compiler performance and retained memory

`MaterialCompilerBenchmark` performs two warm-up and ten measured Release iterations.
The 10,000-record mix contains 8,000 standard-deferred, 1,500 clearcoat complex, and
500 unlit materials. Allocation figures cover global C++ `new`/`new[]` calls and
requested bytes. Retained-byte estimates include inline records plus vector and
string capacities, not allocator bookkeeping.

| Input | Median | p95 | Mean allocations | Mean requested bytes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mixed 10,000 | 25.706 ms | 26.024 ms | 66,501 | 9,832,539 B |
| Sample car, 87 | 0.386 ms | 0.388 ms | 981 | 230,435 B |

The 10,000 compiled results retain approximately 2,736,000 B for standard, 667,500 B
for complex, and 171,000 B for unlit records. The 87 car results retain approximately
71,494 B standard-deferred, 1,074 B standard-forward, and 3,084 B complex-forward.
`SourceMaterial` is 184 inline bytes and `CompiledMaterial` is 248 inline bytes in the
measured x64 build. This is import/change-time work and creates no steady-frame cost.

## Visual, GPU, and memory impact

The compiler is linked but never invoked by the renderer. Therefore the attributable
visual, GPU-time, persistent/transient VRAM, and steady-frame CPU allocation deltas
are zero. The accepted 4K SDR graph capture is rechecked at the end of this slice;
its expected hash remains
`87d130f6c107126d18b2f345a3aaf74c6368ea9ee0f9b8f45174ef67f7107404`.

M2.2 also corrected the M2.1 validator rule for `KHR_materials_volume.thicknessFactor`:
the field is finite and nonnegative, not restricted to one. This evidence change does
not alter any accepted M2.1 fixture result.

## Rollback and next slice

Removing the compiler module and its tests restores the M2.1 state without changing
rendering. No ADR changed; the implementation realizes the already accepted
ADR-0001 F0/F90 and complex-forward decisions.

The next proposed slice is M2.3: mechanically separate immutable compiled materials,
mutable constrained instances, and versioned packed GPU records; add override,
pack/unpack, layout, stale-handle, and diagnostic snapshot tests while keeping the
renderer on the legacy path.
