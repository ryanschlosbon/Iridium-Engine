# M2.6 Complex-Forward Closures

> Historical slice note: M2.10 made canonical routing unconditional and split
> complex closures into depth-writing opaque forward and transparent forward queues.

Status: Complete on 2026-07-22.

This is the durable acceptance record for M2.6 in
`M2-material-compiler-and-closures.md`. It accepts complex-material classification,
GPU records, surface-lobe evaluation, and bounded integration with the retained M1
forward transport. It does not accept M5 clustered lighting or M6 production
transparency, ordering, refraction, absorption, or spectral dispersion.

## Delivered behavior

- `CanonicalComplexForward` is an explicit RHI shader program and Vulkan pipeline
  class. It is not an alias for the legacy forward material.
- Versioned packed material records carry only active tagged clearcoat, sheen,
  anisotropy, iridescence, thin-transmission, volume-transmission, dispersion, and
  diffuse-transmission lobes. Feature bits expose active lobes and unlit state.
- Effective nonzero complex lobes route to canonical forward. Dormant zero-effect
  extensions remain standard deferred with a diagnostic. Unlit routes to the same
  scene-linear forward boundary without evaluating a lighting BSDF.
- The canonical forward shader consumes the complete semantic texture table,
  indexed packed material, shared tangent/normal and standard-BSDF includes, source
  IOR/F0/F90, perceptual roughness, AO, and AP1 scene-linear emissive.
- Clearcoat is layered with coat roughness/normal, sheen adds its grazing lobe,
  anisotropy evaluates oriented anisotropic GGX, and iridescence uses a bounded RGB
  thin-film approximation. Matching CPU reference functions freeze the conventions.
- Thin/volume/diffuse transmission feed source IOR, normal, roughness, thickness,
  attenuation, and color into the retained M1 two-bucket screen transport.
  Metallic transmission is explicitly suppressed and diagnosed. Dispersion data is
  retained but chromatic transport is explicitly deferred to M6.
- The universal three-target GBuffer is unchanged. Complex-only fields live in the
  material buffer and cost shader/forward bandwidth only where complex draws occur.
- Canonical complex materials currently use the existing M1 transparent scheduler.
  Opaque complex materials use opaque blending but the scheduler's read-only depth
  contract. This is a bounded compatibility route, not M5 clustered forward or M6
  general transparent ordering.
- Absence of `--canonical-materials` remains the complete M2 material rollback.
  Per-class approximation and transport limits produce named diagnostics rather than
  silently reducing active complex materials to the standard closure.

## Automated and shader verification

Preset-verified Debug and Release builds compile the new shader with `glslc`; both
configurations pass 20/20 CTest tests. The tests cover:

- every closure class, active feature bit, required lobe type, legal combination,
  dormant-lobe reduction, unsupported/required extension policy, and unlit state;
- all eight packed GPU lobe records, their type order, important parameters, and the
  unchanged 832-byte schema-2 material ABI;
- standard-base agreement plus lobe-on/off differences, finite boundary behavior,
  matching CPU/GLSL anisotropic conventions, and descriptor binding 21;
- explicit iridescence approximation, fixed-light M5, metallic-transmission, and
  volume/dispersion M6 diagnostics;
- the optional car's 87-material snapshot: paint 0/36 are clearcoat complex, lenses
  3/4 are thin-transmission complex, window 6 remains standard forward, both lens
  normal uses reach compiled texture operations, and all compiled car emissive
  factors are exactly zero.

The tracked complex lab compiles to two standard-deferred controls, six complex
materials, and one unlit material. The inactive clearcoat control remains deferred.

## 4K performance, closure work, and memory

Formal timing used Release, visible borderless 3840x2160, validation off, graph
execution, SDR, 500 warm-up frames, 10,000 measured frames, and five independent
warm-steady-state repetitions. Values are median GPU-frame milliseconds per run:

| Scene/path | Run 1 | Run 2 | Run 3 | Run 4 | Run 5 | Median of runs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Complex lab legacy rollback | 0.417856 | 0.418560 | 0.417440 | 0.417824 | 0.417632 | 0.417824 |
| Complex lab canonical | 0.513312 | 0.514016 | 0.513888 | 0.514144 | 0.513920 | 0.513920 |
| Optional car legacy rollback | 0.682848 | 0.683008 | 0.682912 | 0.682624 | 0.682496 | 0.682848 |
| Optional car canonical | 0.930016 | 0.930528 | 0.930624 | 0.930752 | 0.929952 | 0.930528 |

The lab delta is +0.096096 ms. Its complex-forward draw ranges total 0.062464 ms
median; two M1 scene-copy ranges total 0.078848 ms, partly offset by 0.010240 ms less
GBuffer work. The car delta is +0.247680 ms. Its complex/background forward range is
0.304128 ms and foreground forward is 0.013312 ms, while moving coat/lens surfaces
out of the GBuffer saves approximately 0.075 ms. The cost is therefore spatially
attributable to selected forward surfaces and existing two-bucket transport, not a
universal GBuffer expansion.

An additional 10,000-frame pipeline-statistics run recorded:

| Scene | Deferred draws | Standard-forward draws | Complex draws | Unlit draws | Forward fragment invocations | 4K fullscreen equivalents | Unique pipelines |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Complex lab | 2 | 0 | 6 | 1 | 835,945 | 0.100784 | 8 |
| Optional car | 80 | 3 | 4 | 0 | 5,537,289 | 0.667594 | 8 |

The lab uses equal non-overlapping projected triangles, so each forward closure has
approximately 119,421 fragment invocations; six complex closures account for about
716,524 and unlit for about 119,421. The Vulkan query is intentionally pass-scoped,
so the car's aggregate is exact while its per-material pixel split is unavailable;
draws remain exact. The canonical path adds one unique pipeline over the seven-pipeline
rollback. Lobe counters record one of every required lab lobe; the car records two
clearcoats and two thin-transmission lobes.

The complex lab increases requested/committed live engine memory from
763,464,208/768,872,848 to 770,279,952/775,688,592 bytes: exactly 6,815,744 bytes for
the two persistent canonical material buffers, with no GBuffer growth. The car moves
from 849,663,264/855,074,080 to 877,194,240/882,733,856 bytes: +27,530,976 requested
and +27,659,776 committed, comprising the material buffers plus semantic textures
and mip chains the legacy path did not upload. Steady measured-frame C++ allocation
counters remain zero.
